Causal findings
1. Native terrain normals were oriented toward +Y in Y-down model coordinates.
   Treating their X/Z unchanged as lighting's Y-up normal reversed the slopes'
   sun-facing direction. Lighting's correct terrain normal is (-nx,ny,-nz).
   A real hillside pixel (1410,420), triangle19899, had outward geometric sun
   dot -0.0595 but old interpolated lighting dot +0.9990; corrected dot +0.0387.
2. The shadow shader forced visibility0 across an entire terrain triangle when
   geometric dot<=0, while diffuse used the contradictory smoothed normal above.
   This made nearly identical neighboring slopes switch to hard tile-shaped
   darkness. Removing the face-wide gate retains smooth normal interpolation.
   Outward terrain receiver bias remains; prop bias/filtering are unchanged.
3. Terrain's fixed legacy-light relief multiplier was applied after shader sun,
   ambient and independent glow. It supplied a second fixed-direction hill light
   and could modulate emission. Directional Remaster terrain now gets its shape
   from the corrected sun lighting; objects and non-directional paths keep relief.
4. The old terrain diffuse smoothstep followed by two shade smoothsteps saturated
   flat and +/-30/45-degree slopes to the same209/255 at sun85/90 regardless of
   Terrain Shading strength. A terrain-only bounded response now interpolates
   ambient -> prior flat-reference sun shade -> fully sun-facing shade. Low-angle
   sun-facing slopes can brighten beyond the flat reference. Material palette,
   flat endpoints and ambient shadow darkness remain anchored to the old sun
   curve before the intentionally removed fixed legacy multiplier.
   Terrain Shading controls directional slope contrast (strength8 is profile18),
   not a second fixed baked light. No AO/curvature or object aesthetic retuning.
