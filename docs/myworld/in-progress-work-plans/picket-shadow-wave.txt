Problem and correction
----------------------
The prior world-relative near anchor prevents large-coordinate phase resets,
but its orthonormal XY basis still rotates as the sun moves. Eight repeated
native timber/picket quads reproduce a traveling area beat in the near map alone;
a frozen sun is bit-stable. Far caching/history therefore cannot be its sole
cause. Caster-only bilinear alpha did not materially fix it and was rejected.

The High near map now uses a directional sheared projection whose XY rows are
world X + sunX/sunY * world Y and world Z + sunZ/sunY * world Y. Points on a sun
ray retain identical shadow XY, while the texel grid on a horizontal receiver
no longer rotates through repeated cutout details. Depth ordering is unchanged.
Far projection/cache/history, caster alpha cutoff and albedo textures, PCF,
normal bias, resolutions, update cadence, lighting time and gameplay are unchanged.

Camera eye height is not the ground plane. Scene publishes the actual gameplay
focal terrain height as immutable Renderer3DFrame metadata (no wire/map change).
When that reference height changes, near anchor compensation keeps its phase
on whole texels at fixed sun while following the focus within half a texel.
Old frame/camera constructors remain compatible. Finite horizontal-sun fallback
uses the original world-relative orthogonal tracker; supported gameplay sun elevations are >=5 degrees.

Sampling tradeoff: the near half-extent now describes a ground-aligned square
at the focal height, rather than a light-orthogonal square elongated on ground.
High retains the unchanged far map outside that footprint. Low retains its exact previous world-relative orthogonal near tracker,
48-tile near radius and no far map. This is not a universal flicker fix: sparse
binary cutouts can still cross a sampling threshold together, particularly Low.
