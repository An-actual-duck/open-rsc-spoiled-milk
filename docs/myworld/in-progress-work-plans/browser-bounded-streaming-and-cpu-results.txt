Bounded GPU streaming and static CPU reuse: private results, November 12, 2026
============================================================================

Both requested tracks are implemented and tested as explicit private options.
The final combined preview measured about 59.5 fresh FPS in quiet and fixed-NPC
stationary samples on this host, with a much smaller counted GPU allocation than
full residency. Warm walking pauses around 0.3 seconds and multi-second cold
teleport readiness delays remain. This is not seamless travel or ordinary adoption.

The tested bounded mode retains prepared CPU products while keeping GPU
geometry/textures within an explicit budget. Static CPU reuse avoids repeated immutable scene work
and null-model scans. Ordinary client defaults, game content and the public
server are unchanged. Publication of implementation commits is not adoption.

What was implemented
---------------------
Prepared RAM readiness is separated from full GPU pinning without weakening the
exact source/package/catalogue/JAR/material/settings bindings. Java gameplay,
collision, picking and active scope remain authoritative. Bounded GPU mode
admits the complete validated catalogue index, then uploads the required visual
set; metadata admission is not proof of completed GPU coverage. Missing required
resources hold the prior coherent image and block the matching input gate.

The GPU scheduler bounds catalogue count, bytes, fetch/hash preparation, pending
buffers and queues. Required resources precede optional work; terrain is ordered
ahead of scenery. Required resources cannot be evicted. Optional return/adjacent
working sets use spare capacity and preserve a 32 MiB entering reserve. Changes
of source/generation/context cancel stale owned work. Capacity pressure refuses
explicitly rather than dropping coverage or silently raising the selected cap.

The final scheduler shares a target 2 ms, 4 MiB budget across preparation and
upload steps in one tick, with a step-count guard. This is a cooperative budget,
not a promise that an indivisible browser/driver operation always finishes in
2 ms; overruns are measured. Adjacent prefetch starts before a boundary rather
than waiting for the first miss. Held-interval accounting retains honest fresh-
frame gaps instead of resetting the clock and losing the pause from the ring.

Static CPU reuse retains exact revision-scoped override rows, parsed canonical
cell metadata and immutable material object rosters. Ownership includes client,
packet authority, World and exact source snapshots, along with placement and
authority revisions, signed scope, requested plane, presentation base and center.
Roofs and camera-dependent masks stay current. Mixed animated cells and mutable
object overrides retain their original behavior; unknown revisions rebuild.

The bounded canonical identity cache reuses only immutable halo elevations.
Every lookup still rejects any sample touching a loaded gameplay sector, and
reads those mutable heights literally. Exact native source/visual snapshots,
source revision, carrier/offset and current definition/placement values govern
reuse. The final null-request shortcut uses the same private CPU control; every
nonnull and legacy duplicate-removal path is unchanged. Existing native model
kernels, actor order, picking, effects and UI work are not replaced by these caches.

Why the explicit 512 MiB candidate
----------------------------------
The initial 256 MiB proposal is still selectable, but catalogue capacity analysis
found three immutable required views above that cap. With an explicitly assumed
53 MiB local allowance, 62 views would exceed it and the derived maximum is
329.5 MiB. These are catalogue calculations, not measured GPU allocation or an
allocation guarantee. The tested 512 MiB option is explicit; there is no automatic
budget expansion or reduced geometry coverage.

The budget covers counted geometry and world texture allocations, including
local geometry in the shared accounting. It is not total VRAM. Shadows,
framebuffers, sprite/overlay atlases, Worker copies, driver allocations, browser
memory, HTTP/source staging and VM memory are outside that number. A 512-chunk
count cap, 64 MiB pending-data cap and 320 MiB queue-byte cap are separate limits;
a WebCrypto input copy may add up to 64 MiB of transient CPU memory.

The full/OFF stationary run used 658 frames over 16.094 seconds; full/ON used

All four passed fixed-pose/source, visibility and focus checks with zero holds.

not establish a separate stationary FPS benefit from GPU bounding. Each listed cold

speedup. Warm-boundary means were 289.4, 296.6, 300.2 and 289.0 ms in table order.
Those roughly 0.3-second pauses remain. This is not seamless travel or repeated

The final bounded/CPU ON stationary sample at fixed global position (111,624)
recorded 956 fresh backend-submitted world frames over 16.070 seconds:
59.4897 fresh frames/second, no held frames, unchanged pose/source, visible and
focused. This is the quiet forest position reached by the route. The benchmark's
32 fixed NPCs are near (133,660); they are not present at this stationary pose.
It does not by itself establish populated-area or whole-world 60 FPS.

The final bounded/CPU ON route passed actual pointer-driven walking and source-
epoch checks. Its first cold boundary gap was 513 ms, followed by warm/return
boundary maxima of 304, 314, 267, 300 and 260 ms, reconstructed from disjoint
fresh-frame tails with no holes. Setup teleport intervals are excluded. Route
elapsed time includes walking, polling and settling, so is not a loading-only
cost or stationary FPS measure. Held pictures are not fresh gameplay frames.

The first integrated canary had a cold route hold of about 4.2 seconds. That
failure remains preserved under walking-bounded-on-1/ and motivated the shared
tick budget, adjacent prefetch and held-gap accounting correction. The later
513 ms cold route result does not mean all lifecycle holds disappeared. Final
lifetime counters include an initial GPU-coverage hold of 4.242 seconds before
a prior fresh world frame, and an excluded setup-teleport fresh gap of 682 ms.
Startup, teleport and timed walking windows must not be mixed.

Fresh frame counts use the same inner clock and cumulative world counter. The
stationary acceptance check requires no holds and a fixed pose/source, visible
focused browser. Worker/API completion is not physical display scanout or a
per-frame GPU fence. Overlapping interval rings are not concatenated into fake
percentiles. Comparable GPU execution timings are absent unless final receipts
explicitly supply them.

Memory and startup tradeoffs
----------------------------
All four stationary samples reported 411 draw calls and 463,790 triangles.
Equal counts do not prove pixel identity.
The same-pose allocation and host observations are:
  GPU policy   Static CPU   GPU ledger(MiB)   Firefox PSS(MiB)   Firefox RSS(MiB)
  full         OFF          2,155.50          1,912.15            2,746.16
  full         ON           2,155.50          2,097.32            2,923.98
  bounded512   OFF            374.85          1,880.82            2,712.35
  bounded512   ON             374.85          2,148.43            2,979.41

The ledger is about 1.74 GiB smaller with bounded residency on this route. It
measures counted geometry/texture allocations, not per-context physical VRAM
residency or total GPU memory. Host observations are sums from each owned
Firefox tree immediately before closing its cohort. CPU ON has higher PSS in
both pairs; no system-RAM reduction is established. CPU backing retention,
transient state and collection timing differ from GPU accounting. RSS can count
shared pages repeatedly; PSS still covers the whole browser tree, not just Java.
These single observations do not isolate cache cost. Host VRAM counters include
other applications and are not used as per-context allocation proof.

Startup scripts reported 71.91, 70.76, 66.95 and 67.26 seconds to ready in table
order, including login/typing/settle time. These are not clean loader timings.
Complete RAM preparation reported 14.06–14.84 seconds in these runs and remains
a real startup cost; bounding GPU residency is not removal of CPU preparation
or a total memory budget.

The complete retained source/prepared primitive payload remains 275,990,134 bytes
(263.20 MiB): 46,101,734 source-bundle bytes plus 229,888,400 prepared primitive
bytes. It contains 1,797 raw files, 445 activation recipes and 1,330 query cells.
This is a CPU payload lower bound, not Java heap, exact browser RAM or a total
memory cap. It excludes headers/maps/objects, mutable active models, VM/virtual
filesystem copies, queues, transient buffers and browser/driver allocations.
Browser-tree PSS/RSS, when recorded, cover a different scope and must be stated
separately. Startup still validates/decodes this complete CPU preparation; the
bounded GPU option does not remove that initialization cost.

Proof and limits
-----------------
Exact source-bound Java scene/halo differential fixtures cover mutation and
restoration, authoritative absence, mixed animated cells, source/material/options
refusals, ownership replacement, signed fallback, loaded-sector transitions,
carrier/definition changes, bounded cache capacity and counter saturation.
The null-only legal-state differential passed 1,812,738 checks over 6,251 actual
constructed Scene operations plus five private/default-OFF gate combinations.
The original source-bound complete RAM decoder and detached activation checked
all 445 hidden-roof recipes and 64,080 null roof references, then confirmed 720
null removal arguments with 144 nonnull active models. This is not a live-array
snapshot. Additional independent source guards checked 12 unchanged light field-reference
lines and their enclosing guards: four Scene references and eight RSModel
references. These are source-preservation checks, not 12 behavior cases. Native
independent checks report 66,148 checks: 65,848 activation checks, 277 model
checks and 23 policy checks.

Final GPU byte/count/queue/cancellation and ownership regressions have independent
fixtures; mock/synthetic failures are not hardware acceptance. Readiness and
resource-use receipts are required at runtime, not inferred from staged files.
Final signed-scope and pressure smoke passed. Original administrator teleport
inputs visited (639,543,0), (700,441,0), (6,563,-1), (99,705,10), then returned to
(133,660,0). Every target reached actual authoritative scope, source readiness,
fresh frames and a cleared input gate. The geometry/texture ledger stayed within
512 MiB at every poll, peaking near 511.97 MiB; 346 evictions were recorded by
return. During level 10's local fallback, the combined ledger (including retained
catalogue allocations) reached roughly 510 MiB. This illustrates pressure rather
than universal spare capacity. All 445 RAM recipes remained retained. At both
tested nonzero levels, recipe hits and activations stayed at two before and after
the teleport. These are signed fallbacks with retained CPU products, not prepared
residency for extra levels.

The five captures and full poll evidence are preserved under manager
output/diagnostics/browser-bounded-streaming-cpu/scope-final-2/. Time from original
teleport command to ready ranged from about 2.5 to 7.6 seconds in this sequence.
It includes polling and capture overhead, not pure loading cost, but cold
teleports still took several seconds. This is administrator teleport readiness
smoke, not continuous walking latency, a long soak or a pixel-identity oracle.

After return, the final bounded/CPU ON preview at authoritative (133,660,0), with
32 fixed NPCs confirmed, recorded 1,196 fresh world frames over 20.102 seconds:
59.4966 fresh FPS, no holds, fixed pose/source, visible and focused. Its selected
scene reported 427 draw calls and 476,846 triangles. Evidence is under
busy32-final-2/result.json and samples.json in the same manager diagnostic tree.
This is a stationary fixed-NPC, noncombat sample; it does not validate roaming
combat, VFX or a world-wide 60 FPS target. The interval tail still included the
prior teleport pause, so that historical maximum is not a stutter measurement
inside the stationary window.

The final preview remains open for owner exploration with the banner removed
